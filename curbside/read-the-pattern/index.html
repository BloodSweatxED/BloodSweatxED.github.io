The Curbside · Stop 07 · Clinical Pathology

Read the Pattern.

The CBC can move your probability of infection, inflammation, hematologic disease, and illness severity. It rarely tells you the etiology by itself. Read the pattern, not just the WBC.

The curbside question: What is this CBC actually telling me, and what am I pretending it tells me?

The Six-Pass Read.

Stop asking “bacterial or viral?” first
01

Total WBC

High, normal, or low. Then ask whether infection, stress, medications, tissue injury, immune state, or marrow disease fits.

02

Absolute differential

Prioritize ANC and absolute lymphocyte, monocyte, and eosinophil counts. Percentages can mislead.

03

NLR, if useful

ANC divided by absolute lymphocyte count. A stress and inflammation signal. Not an etiologic test. No universal ED cutoff diagnoses bacterial infection or sepsis.

04

Platelets

High can be reactive. Low broadens the problem: sepsis/DIC, viral disease, drugs, ITP, TMA, liver disease, hypersplenism, or marrow pathology.

05

Other cell lines

Anemia plus thrombocytopenia, with or without WBC abnormality, is no longer a simple infection question. Think consumption, hemolysis, marrow, or systemic illness.

06

Morphology

For unexplained, extreme, persistent, or multilineage abnormalities, the smear may be the most useful next test.

WBC + Differential.

Probability modifier, not verdict
High WBC

Leukocytosis

  • Infection or inflammation
  • Stress: pain, seizure, trauma, surgery, exercise
  • Corticosteroids, beta-agonists, epinephrine
  • Smoking or asplenia
  • Tissue injury or necrosis
  • Hematologic malignancy
Normal or low

Do not be reassured

  • Normal does not exclude bacteremia or sepsis
  • Leukopenia can accompany viral infection
  • Severe bacterial infection can also produce leukopenia
  • Consider chemotherapy, marrow suppression, autoimmune disease, and immunosuppression
Absolute count

Neutrophilia / ↑ ANC

Bacterial infection fits. So do stress, trauma, surgery, seizures, steroids, catecholamines, smoking, necrosis, inflammatory disease, and malignancy.

Left shift supports marrow response. It does not prove bacteria.

Smoking usually causes a mild, chronic leukocytosis with neutrophilia. A new count of 30K, toxic change, cytopenias, or systemic illness needs another explanation.

Absolute count

Lymphocyte patterns

Reactive lymphocytosis supports EBV/CMV and other viral syndromes. Pertussis, selected chronic infections, drug reactions, and lymphoproliferative disease can mimic it.

Lymphopenia occurs with stress, sepsis, steroids, COVID-19 and other viral disease, immunosuppression, and malignancy.

Red line

Leukopenia in a toxic patient is not reassuring.

Interpret the count against age, pregnancy, medications, immune status, and baseline.

NLR

Context. Not a trigger.

High NLR can accompany bacterial infection, severe viral infection, trauma, surgery, steroids, malignancy, and physiologic stress. Do not use it alone to diagnose sepsis, start antibiotics, exclude viral disease, or determine disposition.

Travel pearl

Do not stop at “looks viral.”

Fever + leukopenia + thrombocytopenia + compatible travel or exposure should trigger geographically appropriate diagnoses such as dengue.

Don't-Miss Patterns.

These change the question
01

Extreme leukocytosis

  • Severe infection or leukemoid response
  • Steroid or stress response
  • Leukemia or myeloproliferative disease
  • Blasts or unexplained cytopenias: smear + hematologic evaluation
02

Neutropenia + fever

  • Use ANC, not neutrophil percentage
  • Ask about chemo, immunosuppression, medications, recent viral illness, marrow disease
  • Severe neutropenia: ANC <500/µL
  • Fever + significant neutropenia is an infectious emergency
03

Low PLT + anemia

  • Hemolysis and schistocytes?
  • Renal dysfunction?
  • Neurologic findings?
  • Coagulopathy?
  • Think TMA/TTP, DIC, hemorrhage, hemolysis, marrow disease
04

Pancytopenia

  • Marrow
  • Medication
  • Malignancy
  • Infection
  • Sequestration

Common Traps.

The lab is not allowed to diagnose the patient
Pitfall 01

“Normal WBC means bacterial infection is unlikely.”Serious bacterial infection and sepsis can present with a normal WBC.

Pitfall 02

“Neutrophilia means bacteria.”Stress, steroids, trauma, seizures, catecholamines, and smoking can produce the same pattern.

Pitfall 03

“Lymphocytosis means virus.”Pertussis, selected chronic infections, and hematologic disease can mimic it.

Pitfall 04

“Viral illness should cause lymphocytosis.”Important viral illnesses can cause lymphopenia.

Pitfall 05

“NLR > X means bacterial infection or sepsis.”Published thresholds vary. NLR is context, not a trigger.

Pitfall 06

“Zero eosinophils means bacteria.”Eosinopenia occurs with stress and severe infection. It is not specific enough to classify infection.

Pitfall 07

“Monocytosis identifies chronic bacterial infection.”It is nonspecific. Infection, inflammation, recovery, and hematologic disease fit.

Pitfall 08

“The CBC looks viral.”Epidemiology, exam, source evaluation, timing, microbiology, and imaging still decide the case.
