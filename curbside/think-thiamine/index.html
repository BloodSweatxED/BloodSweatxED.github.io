PRN education · The Curbside · Stop 08 · Critical care

Think thiamine.

Unexplained lactate plus nutritional risk? Put thiamine in the initial plan. Treat suspected deficiency while you investigate shock, ischemia, infection, and other causes.

The curbside question: Is this patient missing the cofactor needed to use the glucose we are giving?

A metabolic bottleneck.

Oxygen is only part of the story
Mechanism

Pyruvate needs a way through.

Thiamine diphosphate is a cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase. Deficiency limits conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, impairs oxidative metabolism, and favors lactate production. [1]

Bedside implication

Add it to the differential.

Consider deficiency when lactate is persistent, rising, or disproportionate to the apparent illness, especially with poor intake or malabsorption. It can coexist with shock. A lactate response does not establish the diagnosis.

Red line: resuscitation runs in parallel.

Do not let empiric thiamine delay antibiotics, source control, treatment of hypoperfusion, or evaluation for ischemia and toxicologic causes.

Find the risk at triage.

The history beats the send-out
Low supply / poor absorption

Ask about intake.

  • Malnutrition, prolonged poor intake, or persistent vomiting
  • Alcohol dependence, especially with malnutrition or decompensated liver disease
  • Bariatric surgery or other malabsorption [10]
  • Recent carbohydrate loading after prolonged undernutrition

[4] [5] [6]

Losses / metabolic stress

Check the context.

  • Dialysis or continuous renal replacement therapy, especially with poor intake
  • DKA with nutritional risk or persistent unexplained lactate
  • Marked or prolonged polyuria, including recovery from AKI; review nutrition and replacement
  • Chronic diuretics: a supporting clue, not a diagnosis [11]

Sepsis increases metabolic demand. Sepsis alone does not establish deficiency. [1] [8] [9]

Wernicke encephalopathy

Do not require the triad.

In an at-risk patient, unexplained confusion, abnormal eye movements, or gait dysfunction should trigger consideration of Wernicke encephalopathy. The full triad is often absent. Start parenteral treatment when suspected. [5]

Testing

Plasma is a poor rule-out.

Whole-blood thiamine diphosphate is preferred over plasma or serum testing. Draw before treatment if readily available, but do not delay therapy for the draw or result. A plasma result should not override a convincing presentation. [7]

Treat the syndrome.

Adult dosing · indication matters

Look for: unexplained lactate + credible deficiency risk.

Give parenteral thiamine promptly when deficiency is suspected in a critically ill patient. Assess for Wernicke encephalopathy before choosing a replacement regimen.

Do

Include thiamine in refeeding care.Give it before planned nutrition in high-risk patients and continue with the refeeding protocol. Monitor phosphate, potassium, magnesium, fluid balance, and glucose. Route and dose depend on absorption and clinical status. [6]

Evidence without the oversell.

Replace deficiency. Keep the claims honest.
01 · Prevalence

Common in selected cohorts.

Baseline deficiency was found in 35% of patients in the 2016 septic-shock pilot and 27.4% in TRPSS. These are selected ICU trial populations, not a prevalence estimate for all ED patients or everyone headed to an ICU. [1] [2]

02 · Lactate

A subgroup signal.

The 2016 pilot found no overall improvement in its primary 24-hour lactate outcome. The deficient subgroup had lower 24-hour lactate with thiamine. This supports further targeted study, not blanket treatment of septic shock. [1]

03 · Kidney

Protection is unproven.

TRPSS found no statistically significant improvement in its primary creatinine outcome or in kidney replacement therapy. Favorable numerical trends do not establish prevention of AKI or improved renal recovery. [2]

04 · HAT

A different question.

HAT combines hydrocortisone, ascorbic acid, and thiamine. Negative combination trials, including ACTS, do not answer whether a patient with suspected deficiency needs replacement. They also do not prove benefit from thiamine alone in unselected sepsis. [3]

ED rule: Unexplained or rising lactate + credible deficiency risk → give thiamine promptly, evaluate other causes, and reassess.
Suspected Wernicke → treatment dosing. Hypoglycemia → glucose now.
