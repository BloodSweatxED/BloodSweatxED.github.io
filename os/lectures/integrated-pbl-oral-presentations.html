Three-hour facilitator guide

Integrated PBL: Oral Presentations

Begin with the learner’s problem representation. Use the presentation to expose and improve the reasoning underneath.

Start the sessionBack to desk

Open with presentations

Present the prior case in two minutes. End with a one-sentence problem representation and the leading diagnostic possibilities.

Listeners should record what clarified the case, what obscured it, and what they still need to know.

Run of show

  1. 0-35 minutesOral presentations from the prior case.
  2. 35-55 minutesFeedback and repeat attempts.
  3. 55-70 minutesBreak and reset.
  4. 70-135 minutesProgressive case discussion.
  5. 135-165 minutesSynthesis and management priorities.
  6. 165-180 minutesTransfer task and close.

Listen for structure

Selection

Did the presentation include the facts that change the differential?

Organization

Could the listener follow the clinical story without reconstructing it?

Synthesis

Did the problem representation compress the case without erasing discriminating details?

Commitment

Did the learner state what they think and what remains uncertain?

Feedback, then repeat

  1. Ask the presenter what they were trying to emphasize.
  2. Ask listeners what clinical picture they formed.
  3. Name one detail to cut, move, or sharpen.
  4. Repeat the problem representation immediately.
Facilitator note

Do not let style feedback eclipse reasoning. A polished presentation with a weak synthesis is still a reasoning problem.

Progress the case

Reveal information in stages. Before each reveal, require a commitment.

Frame

What problem are we solving now?

Predict

What finding would most change your ranking?

Update

What moved up, what moved down, and why?

Act

What is the next decision that cannot wait?

Close with transfer

Give the final case summary in one minute. State the leading explanation, the strongest competing explanation, and the next action.

End by naming one presentation habit each learner will use in the next case.

After class

  • Which feedback produced an immediate improvement?
  • Where did presentation structure reveal a reasoning gap?
  • What case reveal should move or change next time?
