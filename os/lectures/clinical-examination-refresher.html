Clinical Examination Refresher

The examination is not a recital. It is a sequence of tests chosen to change a clinical decision.

Run the sessionBack to desk

Session target

By the end, learners should be able to choose a focused examination from the differential, describe findings precisely, and state what each finding changes.

Facilitator move: After every finding, ask, “What does that do to your next decision?”

Case 1: Shortness of breath

An adult presents with sudden dyspnea. The initial data allow several plausible cardiopulmonary explanations.

  1. Ask learners to name the two diagnoses they most need to separate.
  2. For each proposed examination element, require a predicted finding.
  3. Ask how a positive or negative finding changes the next test, treatment, or level of care.

Debrief: A useful focused examination is conditional. The working differential determines the maneuver, and the result must connect to action.

Case 2: Weakness and dizziness

An older adult reports generalized weakness and unsteadiness. The history does not yet separate circulatory, neurologic, medication-related, and functional causes.

  1. Ask what must be observed before the patient is labeled “nonfocal.”
  2. Have learners compare a recumbent examination with findings during sitting, standing, and walking when safe.
  3. Require a one-sentence report that distinguishes what was tested from what was inferred.

Debrief: Function is part of the examination. A normal result at rest may not answer the disposition question.

Language cleanup

  • Replace “neuro intact” with the specific domains examined and relevant findings.
  • Replace “appears well” with observable features such as work of breathing, perfusion, interaction, and mobility.
  • Replace “benign abdomen” with the findings that address the working concern.
