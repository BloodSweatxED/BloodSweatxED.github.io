Two-hour facilitator guide

Teams and Teamwork

Effective teams make roles, priorities, uncertainty, and closed-loop communication visible.

Start the sessionBack to desk

Opening reflection

Think of a team that was technically capable but did not function well. What was missing?

Collect observable breakdowns. Separate individual competence from team performance.

Run of show

  1. 0-15 minutesOpening reflection and shared definition.
  2. 15-40 minutesAnalyze a team breakdown.
  3. 40-65 minutesPractice closed-loop communication.
  4. 65-75 minutesBreak.
  5. 75-105 minutesTeam scenario and structured debrief.
  6. 105-120 minutesTransfer to the next clinical team.

Four team functions

Roles

Who owns each task, decision, and escalation?

Priorities

Does everyone know what matters most right now?

Communication

Are requests directed, acknowledged, and confirmed?

Adaptation

Does the team update its plan when the situation changes?

Closed-loop communication

  1. Direct the request to a named person.
  2. The receiver repeats the request.
  3. The sender confirms or corrects it.
  4. The receiver announces completion or a barrier.
Practice prompt

Run the same short task twice. The first round uses ordinary communication. The second requires directed requests and explicit closure. Compare what changed.

Debrief the system

Facts

What happened, in order, without interpretation?

Frames

What did different team members think was happening?

Friction

Where did the team lose information or coordination?

Fix

What one system change would improve the next attempt?

Transfer task

Before your next team task, make one role, one priority, and one escalation trigger explicit.

Ask learners to write the exact sentence they will use.

After class

  • Which scenario exposed the clearest team failure mode?
  • What communication habit changed the second attempt?
  • What would make the next debrief more candid?
